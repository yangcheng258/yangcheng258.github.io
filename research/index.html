Research

Two threads, one question.

My research sits at the meeting point of two questions: how to measure what makes a rural place worth living in, and how to measure what counts as green work. One thread runs on community-capitals and spatial methods at the U.S. county scale; the other on bottom-up, NLP-built measures of green tasks and skills, applied to wage and labor-market questions.

Theme01

Rural Livability & Quality of Life

How can community-capitals data plus resident judgment be combined into spatially-informed measures of well-being at the county level? This thread compares seven index-construction techniques on a single 3,100-county dataset and reframes livability from amenity-shopping toward "place capacity" — what places possess, not just what people choose. With Tessa Conroy and Steven Deller (UW–Madison).

Theme02

Workforce & the Green Economy

What counts as a green job, and where are green skills concentrated? This thread builds bottom-up greenness measures from O*NET task content using natural language processing — the Task Greenness Score and the Occupational Greenness Potential (OGP) Index — and uses them to track the green wage premium from 2006 through 2024. With Susan Chen and Andrew Katz (Virginia Tech).

Theme03

Migration & Place Choice

How do life-cycle stage, housing market conditions, and place characteristics shape where people move and why? A newer thread that takes the spatial-livability framing into household decisions over the life course — pulling from the life-course migration, housing-constraint, and demographic-subgroup literatures.
