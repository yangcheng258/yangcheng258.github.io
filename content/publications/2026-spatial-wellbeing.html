As well-being gains traction as an economic development strategy, scholars and practitioners face two key challenges: the large number of factors associated with community well-being and the heterogeneity of places, which necessitates a local perspective on these relationships. Following Isserman et al. (2009), we construct a U.S. county-level well-being index using four well-being indicators. We then apply Bayesian model averaging to select from 77 candidate community variables, organized within the Community Capitals Framework, and estimate a geographically weighted regression to assess whether these associations vary across space. We find that the variables most associated with community well-being span four capital types: human, social, financial, and natural, with human capital measures dominating. We also find most community variables show significant spatial variation. Together, these findings suggest that policies aimed at improving community well-being should prioritize investments in people while remaining responsive to local assets and contextual conditions.
