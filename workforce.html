Research theme · 02

Workforce & the green economy

What counts as a green job, and where are green skills concentrated in the U.S. labor market? My dissertation built bottom-up greenness measures from O*NET task content using natural language processing — the Task Greenness Score and the Occupational Greenness Potential (OGP) Index. The current line of work uses those measures to track the green wage premium from 2006 through 2024 and to map green-skill exposure across regions, including the Appalachian coal-mining communities that may bear the heaviest transition costs.

Tasks, Skills, and Jobs in the Green Economy

Cheng, Y. (advisor: Susan E. Chen)
Ph.D., Virginia Tech, Department of Agricultural & Applied Economics.Published
PDF DOI Code Cite
›

Three-chapter dissertation. Chapter 1 builds the Task Greenness Score and the Occupational Greenness Potential (OGP) Index from O*NET task content via NLP and clustering, identifying five occupation groups and a ~3% unconditional green wage premium for 2019–2020. Chapter 2 maps green-skill exposure across the U.S. with a focus on Appalachian coal-mining communities. Chapter 3 examines green ARRA stimulus and finds it negatively correlated with wages and job creation, with cross-region manufacturing spillovers. Committee: Andrew Katz, Suqin Ge, Jeffrey Alwang.
