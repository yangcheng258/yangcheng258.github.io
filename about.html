About

A note on who I am.

A regional economist working at the intersection of rural livability, workforce transition, and the small American communities that often get left out of the national conversation.

Based inMadison, WI AffiliationUW–Madison · Ag & Applied Econ RolePostdoctoral Research Associate

I'm a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Agricultural & Applied Economics at UW–Madison. My research sits at the meeting point of two questions: how to measure what makes a rural place worth living in, and how to measure what counts as green work. One thread runs on community-capitals and spatial methods at the U.S. county scale; the other on bottom-up, NLP-built measures of green tasks and skills.

On rural livability, I work with co-authors at the UW–Madison Rural Livability Project — Tessa Conroy, Steven Deller, Erin Gaede, Mckenzie Boyce — to compare seven index-construction techniques across roughly 3,100 contiguous U.S. counties, build the WIndicator dashboard for Wisconsin county boards, and study how housing financial stress and food insecurity compound to shape rural health. The methods paper is in Papers in Regional Science; companion working papers target Regional Studies and the RSS rural-livability special issue.

On the green-economy side, I built the Occupational Greenness Potential (OGP) Index from O*NET task content using natural language processing during my Ph.D. at Virginia Tech (advisor Susan E. Chen). I'm currently using OGP to track the green wage premium from 2006 through 2024, in collaboration with Susan Chen and Andrew Katz. I'm always interested in talking with practitioners and researchers working on similar questions; the contact page has the relevant addresses.
